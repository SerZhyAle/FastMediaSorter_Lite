Privacy Policy

Last updated: 8 November 2026

Fast Media Sorter & Sharing for Windows collects no personal data. There is no advertising, and no telemetry or analytics. It runs entirely on your own device, and the developer receives no data about you or your use of the app - unless you choose to send your logs yourself (see Diagnostic logs) - honestly, we wouldn't know what to do with it anyway. Where the app signs in anywhere - the password of a share server, an exchange account, a translation provider's key - it signs in to a server or service you chose; no account is ever created with, or reported to, the developer.

What the app accesses, and why

  • Your media files. The app reads, displays, copies, moves, renames and deletes the image and video files in the folders you open - that is its core purpose. It only ever touches files and folders you point it at (including network shares you browse to). It has seen your meme folder and judged nothing.
  • Servers and network shares you add yourself. A folder on an SFTP server, or a network share that needs a login, is opened only after you add it (by hand, or by importing the file the Share Manager makes). The app then opens a direct, outbound connection to the address you gave it and to nothing else - it never listens for connections for this and never contacts the developer. Three small things are kept on your PC, in your Windows account: the server's address and user name, the server's SSH key fingerprint (so a server pretending to be yours is refused), and the password, encrypted with Windows (DPAPI) so it can be read only by you on this PC. If you untick "Remember the password (protected by Windows)", the password is held in memory until the program closes and is never written anywhere. Files you open from a server are copied into a private cache folder that is deleted when you leave the resource or close the program. Passwords are never included in the logs package you may choose to send.
  • Folder sharing - optional, opt-in. If you enable it, the bundled Share Manager companion runs a local SFTP server so your own phone can browse the folders you choose. The access password and the server host key are generated and kept only on your PC, and files are served directly between your own devices - never through us or any third party. While a share is on, any device on your current network (home, office, or a public Wi-Fi) can reach the PC on the share port; access needs the password embedded in the QR/config you share, so treat that code as a key and do not post it publicly. A LAN only switch keeps the share off the internet; idle connections close after a few hours, and you can cap how many devices connect at once. Starting a share also reaches out, with no extra switch to turn on: the worker asks two public services, api.ipify.org and icanhazip.com, which address the internet sees for your PC (they see your public IP address and nothing else), and this happens even with LAN only on. Unless LAN only is on (it is off by default), the worker also asks your own router to forward the share port (UPnP or NAT-PMP) and, once a forwarded port exists, asks check-host.net to confirm that it answers from outside, which reveals your public address and port to that service for the check. While a share runs the PC announces itself on your local network (mDNS) so a paired phone can find it again when its address changes; that stays inside your network. Turning folder sharing on needs one administrator approval, either in the installer or the first time you enable it in the app (Windows asks): it adds a firewall rule that lets only the sharing program accept connections, and uninstalling removes it (the Microsoft Store package declares that rule in its manifest instead). The app keeps local-only connection counters (this is not telemetry and never leaves your PC).
  • Broadcasts for StreamsPlayer. In the 64-bit viewer, right-click a video or audio file or its folder and choose Broadcast. Share Manager exports a .spres file, QR code or quick link for import in StreamsPlayer. A folder is one playlist channel with a frozen list; Refresh replaces that list and Revoke stops its link. The HTTP gateway has its own switch, off by default. Each token grants read-only access to one broadcast and normally expires after 24 hours. HTTP is unencrypted: share the document privately and keep internet access off unless you need it.
  • Live broadcast and the camera - only after you start them. The Share Manager's live broadcast captures the one source you choose: a screen, one window, a camera, the microphone or the system audio. Nothing is captured before you press "Start the broadcast"; "Stop the broadcast" ends it, and closing a captured window ends its broadcast too. Everything visible or audible in that source reaches the listeners, private content included, so pick one window rather than the whole screen when other things are open. The listeners are viewers on your own network when "Allow viewers on my network" is ticked and, when this PC is joined to an exchange account, listeners reached through that account's exchange server (see the exchange account item below); nothing goes to the developer. Treat the broadcast's link, code or .fmsbcast file, and the listener password you set, as a key; "New link password" gives the link a new password while you are on air. "Scan with camera.." in the phone code window and the exchange account window reads a phone's QR code through the first camera of this PC: its frames stay in memory, and that window saves and sends nothing. Windows itself decides whether programs may use the camera and the microphone (Settings → Privacy & security → Camera, Microphone); the Microsoft Store package declares the camera and microphone capabilities for these features.
  • FileDO disks - optional, opt-in. If you connect a .fdd container for sharing, the server mounts it on this PC and publishes its contents to authorized SFTP clients. A selected Windows drive letter is a local alias for the current signed-in session; it does not create an SMB share. An encrypted disk is unlocked on the PC. Its password is sent only to the verified local worker and FileDO, never in command arguments or logs. Disk autostart is a separate choice: only after explicit consent is its credential stored in protected Windows storage on this PC. Disabling autostart or removing the disk from sharing deletes that stored key. Mounting requires administrator rights; enabling ordinary folder sharing does not silently elevate the server or change a service account. See the FileDO disk sharing guide.
  • Always-on sharing - a Windows service, only if you ask for it. It comes either from the separate Server edition download or from the Share Manager's own "Switch sharing to a Windows service.." (Manager settings → Hosting); it is never installed by default, and either way Windows asks for administrator approval first. The same folder sharing is then hosted by a Windows service instead of by your signed-in session, so the folders you chose stay reachable from boot even when nobody is logged on. Nothing about what is collected changes - there is still no telemetry, and files still go straight between your own devices - but the exposure window does: the share is up whenever the machine is, not only while you are using it. The service runs as LOCAL SERVICE, which is given read access to each folder you share (and write access only where you allowed writing) and nothing else. Its host key, password and settings live in a machine folder, %ProgramData%\FastMediaSorterCompanion, that only administrators, the service and your account can open. "Remove the server role" in the Share Manager, or uninstalling the Server edition, stops and removes the service, takes back those folder permissions and removes the firewall rule; uninstalling the ordinary edition offers to do the same for a service its Share Manager set up (a silent uninstall, such as winget uninstall, cannot ask and leaves it in place). The host key is kept unless you delete that folder, so your paired phones still work if you reinstall.
  • An exchange account - a server somebody runs; normally, you. The 64-bit viewer and the Share Manager can join an exchange account on an exchange server, so that every device of one account sees the folders the others publish. The server is not ours: it is a small program somebody runs - normally you, on your own PC or a machine you control. Until you sign in with the login and password of an account on that server (or with a pairing code that one of its devices made for you), nothing happens: no exchange connection, no listening port, nothing written. The password is typed once and is not kept - unless you switch on keeping it, and then it is encrypted with Windows (DPAPI) for your Windows account in one local file, while the server itself stores only a hash of it, never the password. An enrolled device keeps a device token instead of the password, encrypted the same way on your PC; the server stores only a hash of the token. The app remembers the server's address and its certificate fingerprint, and refuses a server whose certificate ever changes. A connection string that another device of the account can show you is that address with the fingerprint: it carries no password and no token, and it never enters the logs or the support archive. What the exchange server sees, and what it does not. The exchange server connects your devices, so it knows the names of your account, devices, folders and broadcasts, sees which devices are online, and how much is sent and when. It cannot open what goes through it: folders and their files, broadcasts and the clipboard are encrypted on your device and opened only on your other devices or by the people you gave access to. To look inside, whoever runs the server would have to add a device of their own to your account, and it would appear in your list of devices. Via Google Drive, files and the clipboard are kept in your own Google account, like your other files on Google Drive; no exchange server takes part. The server stores password and token hashes, device public keys and published-share records on disk. Sealed share access, broadcast/access-grant key boxes and clipboard values stay in memory; sealed payloads and keys do not belong in its logs. Clipboard transfers. Choose "Send the clipboard to the server" in Share Manager, its tray menu or its desktop shortcut to send the current text, image or one file to each joined member account. "Get the clipboard from the server" retrieves its newest value. This transfer is explicit, not continuous clipboard monitoring. What is sent is exactly what is on the clipboard at that moment, a password you copied included: the program does not inspect, filter or mask it. The server holds only the last complete value in memory, never on disk. A new complete upload replaces it; an interrupted upload leaves the previous value. Restarting the server or deleting the account clears it. Logs record only the kind and size class, never the clipboard contents or file name. Outside visibility check. "Is my server visible from outside" explicitly asks check-host.net to check the public host and port; that third party receives the endpoint you asked it to check.
  • Leaving or deleting an exchange account. "Sign out on this device" in Share Manager's exchange account settings forgets this PC's sign-in; it does not delete the account or sign out its other devices. To delete the account, use "Delete my account" on its server card in the Android app's My devices page and confirm with the account password, or ask the server operator to remove it. If you run the server, remove the account from its administration page in Share Manager. Deletion erases the password hash, enrolled devices and token hashes (including revoked-device records), published folder and broadcast records, outstanding pairing codes, access grants to and from the account, and its in-memory clipboard. Active sessions end. Source files and copies already delivered are not erased; files in your Google Drive and operational logs kept for the operator's configured retention period remain. Uninstalling the app does not delete an account.
  • Network - only when you use a feature that needs it. The on-image translation feature is off until you enable and configure it. When you use it, the app sends the recognized text to the translation endpoint you choose - a local Ollama model on your own machine, or a LibreTranslate server you specify - to receive the translated text. OCR (text recognition) runs fully offline. Apart from that, the app makes no network requests except to fetch an optional component you ask for: additional OCR language packs from the public Tesseract data repository; the OCR and video playback runtimes from nuget.org, when a build did not bring them along; FFmpeg - the video encoder behind "Replace with video" - from its published Windows builds; the Ollama installer from ollama.com, and translation models through your own Ollama, when you press the button for them; and doc-html-translate through winget, when you press its install button. Each happens only after you press that button or say yes to a prompt that names what is being fetched; nothing about your files, your paths or your usage is sent with any of them. If a runtime needs the Microsoft Visual C++ Redistributable and it is missing, the app says so and can open Microsoft's download in your browser - it never downloads or runs that installer itself.
  • API keys (if you set one). If a translation provider you configure needs an API key, it is stored encrypted on your device using Windows DPAPI and is sent only to that provider's endpoint when translating.

Local files the app writes

  • Settings. Preferences and hotkeys are stored in the Windows registry (in the Microsoft Store build these live inside the app's package container).
  • Settings import backups (64-bit program). Before importing a profile, the app saves your current preferences, including folder histories and playback positions, in a protected folder under %LOCALAPPDATA%\SZA\FastMediaSorter\settings-backups (inside the app's local data root in the Microsoft Store build). These local rollback copies exclude passwords and API keys. The import stops if a private backup cannot be created; nothing is saved beside the imported file or in a shared temporary folder. Backups are not deleted automatically. You can restore one through Settings → Import settings, or delete old copies yourself when you no longer need them.
  • OCR data and caches. Downloaded Tesseract language packs, a downloaded copy of FFmpeg, the translation cache and the decode cache (decoded copies of the WEBP, AVIF and HEIC files you have opened, kept so the second view is instant) are stored under %LOCALAPPDATA%\SZA\FastMediaSorter. These never leave your device, and both caches can be emptied or switched off in Settings.
  • Viewing history (64-bit program). In the same registry place as the settings, the viewer keeps the lists of recent files and folders (up to the limits you set), the zoom of a folder when you choose to remember zoom per folder, and, for video, the playback position of each file - its path, size, modification time and position - so a video resumes where you stopped. "Remember the playback position" switches the last one off. A file opened from an archive or from a server is never remembered by its temporary copy.
  • Archives (64-bit program). A ZIP, CBZ, 7z or RAR you open is shown as a folder: its pictures and videos are unpacked one by one into a private folder under %LOCALAPPDATA%\SZA\FastMediaSorter\archive-cache while you browse it. The folder is deleted when you leave the archive or close the program, and anything a crash left behind is swept the next time you open an archive.
  • Editing a picture (64-bit program). When the editor saves over the original, it first writes the result beside it under a temporary name that contains .fms-tmp (for example photo.fms-tmp.jpg) and swaps it in only when it is complete, so a failure cannot leave half a photo. The rest of the original's EXIF is carried over except the embedded preview thumbnail, which would show the picture from before the edit.
  • Exports that carry a key. "Export.." in the Resource Manager (64-bit program) writes a .fmscfg file to the place you choose, readable only by your account; it holds the server's address and user name, and the password only if you pick that option. The Share Manager's "Send by email.." hands its .fmscfg (the share's access key) to your mail program through a temporary copy in a private folder that exists only for that call and is deleted right after. Treat such a file as you would a password.

Data sharing

None with the developer - the kind of "none" that is actually none, and no telemetry or analytics ever. The only things that leave your device do so at your explicit choice and go where you direct them, not to us: the text you translate goes to the translation endpoint you configured; shared files go directly to your own phone over SFTP; the folders of an exchange account, and the clipboard value when you press its send button, pass through the exchange server you chose, exactly as described above; when a share starts, your public IP address is seen by api.ipify.org and icanhazip.com (which return it to the program) and, unless LAN only is on, your public address and port are shown to check-host.net solely to test reachability; and the log archive reaches the developer only if you send it yourself from your own mail program.

This website

The program itself contacts none of the addresses below. They belong to the product website - the home page, the guides and the install-trust page - and your browser contacts them when it opens those pages. This privacy page loads nothing from any of them. No analytics, tag-manager or advertising address is contacted by any page of the site.

  • fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com (Google Fonts) - the stylesheet and the two font files of the site's lettering, loaded on the pages that use it. They receive your IP address and your browser's user agent, as any web server does; nothing else is sent.

Diagnostic logs

The app writes a plain-text log of its own work next to the program (or, when that folder is not writable, under your local application data). It never leaves your device on its own. Settings → About has a "Send the logs to the author" button: it packs those logs and a report of your version, Windows details and settings into an archive, shows you what went in, and opens a new message in your own mail program with the archive attached. Nothing is transmitted until you send that message yourself, and you can open the archive and inspect it first. The logs contain the paths and names of the files you opened; your translation API key is never included.

Children's privacy

The app collects no data from anyone, including children.
